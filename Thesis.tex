\begin{abstract}

    Tourism plays a fundamental role in the economic development of many Italian cities, including \textbf{Ascoli Piceno}, a destination with significant untapped potential. Despite its rich cultural, historical, and gastronomic heritage, the city has yet to establish itself as a highly competitive tourist destination compared to other Italian locations.

    This project analyzes tourism data from various Italian cities to identify key trends in visitor segmentation, pricing models, and market positioning. To address this challenge, this study seeks to answer the following research question: "\textbf{\textit{What is the optimal combination of pricing, positioning, and promotional strategies for Ascoli Piceno to increase tourism competitiveness?}}". By combining secondary data analysis with primary research through surveys, the project provides insights into tourist preferences, spending behavior, and the effectiveness of different promotional strategies. Additionally, financial factors—including cost structures, potential partnerships, and the role of influencer marketing—are examined to understand their impact on tourism promotion.
        
    Using clustering techniques and strategic pricing models, the research proposes optimized tourism packages tailored to different market segments. The findings aim to offer evidence-based recommendations for Ascoli Piceno to improve its tourism appeal, increase visitor numbers, and strengthen its overall economic sustainability. Further research could explore the long-term effects of these strategies and identify additional factors that influence the competitiveness of tourist destinations.
\end{abstract}

\section{Tourism on a Global Scale}

\indent \textbf{Tourism} is recognized as one of the most important economic sectors in the world. In 2019, it represented a significant share of the global economy, accounting for \textbf{7\% of global trade}. However, despite its importance, tourism was also among the sectors \textbf{most severely impacted by the Covid-19 pandemic}, which affected not only global economies but also livelihoods, public services, and development opportunities in countries across all continents \cite{unwtocovid}. Now, four years after the onset of the pandemic, the sector has shown strong signs of recovery. International tourist arrivals have returned to \textbf{99\% of 2019 levels}, signaling that tourism has bounced back from what is considered the worst crisis in its history \cite{unwtoppt}. As shown in \textit{Figure 1.1}, in addition to the rebound in arrivals, there has also been a notable rise in international tourism revenues. Total international tourism receipts reached \textbf{USD 1.7 trillion in 2024}, reflecting a 10.5\% increase compared to 2023 and a 14.1\% increase from 2019 in real terms \cite{unwtodash}.

In the case of \textbf{Africa}, the continent received \textbf{7\% more} tourists than it did before the pandemic began, indicating a solid recovery. \textbf{Europe}, known as the world’s largest destination region, also showed positive results by recording \textbf{1\% more} international tourist arrivals than in 2019. Meanwhile, the \textbf{Americas} managed to recover \textbf{97\%} of its pre-pandemic tourist numbers, which is just a \textbf{3\% decrease compared to 2019}. Finally, the region of \textbf{Asia and the Pacific} reached \textbf{87\%} of its 2019 levels, showing a \textbf{13\% decline} in international arrivals compared to the pre-pandemic period \cite{unwtoppt}.

\section{The Italian Context}

\subsection{International Tourism and Overnight Stays}

\indent \textbf{Italy} continues to be a an important destination for international tourism, ranked among the \textbf{top 5} countries in the world, behind Türkiye who had 60.6 million visitors (\textit{see Figure 1.4}). The country counts with a rich cultural heritage, natural beauty, and culinary excellence. However, recent trends show a mixed picture in terms of volume and economic impact. According to the United Nations World Tourism Organization (UNWTO), Italy recorded \textbf{57.8 million} international tourist arrivals in 2024, which reflects a \textbf{10.4\% decrease compared to 2019}. Despite the decline in visitor numbers, the country experienced a \textbf{strong financial recovery}, with international tourism receipts reaching \textbf{USD 58.7 billion}, an \textbf{18.5\% increase over 2019} \cite{unwtodash}. This highlights that the country has \textbf{not yet fully returned to its pre-pandemic levels} in terms of international arrivals, suggesting that there is still significant room for growth and strategic interventions aimed at increasing the level of tourism.

\begin{figure}[ht]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=1\textwidth]{Images/country_rank.png}
    \captionsetup{font=footnotesize}
    \caption{Ranking of International Arrivals by Country. Source: UNWTO.}
    \label{fig:country_rank}
\end{figure}

\indent Tourism in Italy is also measured by the number of \textbf{overnight stays in accommodations}, where the country positioned itself as the second-largest European destination in terms of total nights spent. According to the Italian National Institute of Statistics (ISTAT), Italy recorded \textbf{458.4 million overnight stays in 2024}, representing a \textbf{2.5\% increase compared to 2023}. Among these, \textbf{foreign tourists} accounted for around \textbf{250 million nights}, a 6.8\% increase, while domestic stays declined by 2.2\%, totaling roughly 208 million nights. Foreign visitors now represent \textbf{54.6\% of all overnight stays}, indicating that international tourism has regained its leading role in the Italian tourism sector. This trend became especially evident in the fourth quarter of 2024, when total overnight stays grew by 11.1\%, mainly driven by international travelers (+15.6\%), who outnumbered domestic tourists in that quarter for the first time \cite{istat_q4}.

\subsection{Spending and Economic Contribution}

\indent According to the first edition of the “Tourism and Incoming Watch” report, developed by Nexi in collaboration with the National Tourism Observatory of the Ministry of Tourism, foreign travellers spent over €20.9 billion in Italy in 2024 using \textbf{payment cards} alone, representing a substantial \textbf{increase of 37.9\% compared to 2022}. This figure reflects not only the volume of international tourism but also the growing role of digital payments in capturing economic value across the tourism sector.

\indent The report also highlighted a notable diversification in the origin of \textbf{high-spending tourists}. Travellers from Brazil and Australia showed the largest increases in card-based spending, with growth rates of +155\% and +100\%, respectively. Meanwhile, US tourists remained the most significant contributors in absolute terms, with more than €3.8 billion spent, accounting for 18.3\% of total foreign tourist expenditure. Tourists from Saudi Arabia (€913) and the United Arab Emirates (€822) had the highest average card spend per transaction, both nearly double the international average of €411.

\indent In terms of spending categories, almost \textbf{half (49.6\%)} of all international tourist expenditure in Italy went to \textbf{restaurants and accommodation services}. \textbf{Fashion and accessories} accounted for another \textbf{12.2\%}, with visitors from Arab countries and Southeast Asia allocating an above-average share of their budgets to jewellery and department store shoppings.

\indent The geographic distribution of tourist spending also reveals concentration patterns. Just \textbf{20 provinces generated 75\% of all foreign tourist value}, confirming the dominance of Italy’s major cultural and urban destinations: \textbf{Rome, Milan, Venice, Florence, and Naples}. These cities continue to attract visitors and generate sustained economic benefits for the national economy.

\indent Among the most popular travel routes, the \textbf{Rome – Naples and Pompeii – Amalfi Coast} circuits generated the highest overall turnover for the third consecutive year, with a \textbf{31\% increase} over the 2022–2024 period. However, the \textbf{Rome – Florence – Venice} itinerary recorded the highest growth rate in the same period, rising by \textbf{+69.2\%}. This trend suggests that traditional cultural circuits remain dominant, but their internal dynamics are shifting due to changing tourist preferences and purchasing behaviors.

\indent Another key finding is the \textbf{decline in tourism seasonality}. Thanks to diversified travel flows and evolving preferences, international visitor demand is now \textbf{more evenly distributed throughout the year}. This has reinforced Italy’s profile as a \textbf{year-round destination}, which contributes to greater economic resilience and offers new opportunities for regional development beyond the summer peak.

\section{Ascoli Piceno}

\indent \textbf{Ascoli Piceno} is a historic city located in the \textbf{Marche region of central Italy}, known for its rich cultural heritage, well-preserved medieval architecture, and local traditions. It is a city of ancient origins, once a strategic Roman center along the Via Salaria. Known as the \textbf{“City of a Hundred Towers”}, it has one of the most elegant and well-preserved historical centers in Italy, largely built in travertine stone. The city is renowned for its harmonious urban layout and architectural beauty, especially visible in its iconic squares, \textbf{Piazza del Popolo and Piazza Arringo}, which serve as central hubs of civic and cultural life. Rich in Romanesque, Gothic, and Renaissance heritage, Ascoli offers an authentic and immersive experience in tradition. Its compact, walkable layout and local identity make it an ideal example of high-quality, culture-oriented tourism, away from mainstream destinations \cite{italia_it}.

\subsection{Touristic Situation}

\indent Although it experienced a \textbf{significant increase in 2024 of +18\% in tourist arrivals} compared to pre-pandemic levels in 2019, \textbf{Ascoli Piceno still shows clear potential for further growth in tourism}. Among Italy’s 5,014 municipalities available in the dataset of ISTAT (Istituto Nazionale di Statistica), Ascoli Piceno ranked \textbf{544th in 2024}, recording a total of \textbf{43,873 arrivals}. This figure, while reflecting a notable improvement, remains modest when positioned within the national context. For example, when compared to the top 10 destinations by arrivals in the same year, \textbf{Ascoli Piceno represents only 0.4\% of the total arrivals of Rome}, the top-ranked city \cite{istat}.

\indent Furthermore, as shown in \textit{Figure 2.5}, it is important to highlight the \textbf{high concentration of tourism flows} among a small group of urban destinations. In fact, the \textbf{top four cities alone accounted for 19\% of all tourist arrivals in Italy in 2024}, demonstrating the uneven distribution of tourism across the country. Additionally, \textbf{the following six cities registered more than one million arrivals each}, underlining the dominance of a few key destinations \cite{istat}. This disparity reinforces the relevance of studying smaller but culturally rich destinations like Ascoli Piceno, which, despite their lower absolute numbers, contribute to the diversification and sustainability of Italian tourism.

\subsection{Overtourism: Ascoli Piceno's Opportunity?}

\indent Europe has been called the world’s museum, but its record numbers of visitors have also made it ground zero for concerns about overtourism. Among factors driving the record numbers are cheap flights, social media, the ease of travel planning using artificial intelligence \cite{apnews_ot}. This matter is something that is not new, and many residents of the affected cities started a time ago with anti-tourism protests, even a new wave of anti-tourism protests is being planned across southern Europe later for this summer (2025) \cite{bbc_ot}. Furthermore, the potential culprits point the finger at each other. For example, Airbnb says hotels are driving the problem, while Tui, Europe’s largest hotel operator, has hit back, saying Airbnb is to blame \cite{bloomberg_ot}.

\indent In Italy, 70\% of tourists go to only 1\% of Italy’s territory \cite{itsegreta_ot}. There are several destinations in the country at risk of overtourism, including popular cities like Rome, Florence, Milan, and Venice, as well as lesser-known cities such as Bolzano, Livorno, and Rimini. According to a study organized by Demoskopika, Bolzano, Livorno, Naples, Rimini, Trento, and Venice are at a high-level risk of overtourism. Following them, at a high-level of overtourism are Aosta, Florence, Forlì-Cesena, Gorizia, Grosseto, Imperia, La Spezia and the Cinque Terre, Milan, Ravenna, Rome, Savona, and Trieste. Taking these into account, there is an immediate need for responsible actions to ensure that tourism contributes to cultural enrichment and economic development without burdening future generations \cite{trn_ot}. Taking these into account, and according to Visit Italy CEO and founder Ruben Santopietro, “99\% of the destinations in Italy have an incredible capacity to grow”. Nevertheless, this can backfire, as cities with small populations and little tourism infrastructure can be woefully unprepared for hordes of people \cite{itsegreta_ot}.

\indent Nowadays, the sustainable tourism, term first started to be batted around in the 1990s \cite{itsegreta_ot}, is gaining terrain in this field. Many destinations are responding with new ideas for a more sustainable travel season. For example, Switzerland's national train network will be run completely on hydroelectric power this year (2025), as part of its new Swisstainable strategy that aims to distribute tourism more evenly across the country and calendar. The Swisstainable website offers 33\% off public transport when booking a sustainability-certified Swisstainable hotel, or 25\% off for any stay in a Swisstainable hotel. On the other hand, in Spain they remove nearly 66,000 unregulated Airbnb listings and pulled back on social media influencer campaigns that bring selfie tourists to its small and easily overwhelmed beaches. Also, they developed a digital platform that monitors how many people are on beaches, as well as the air quality, sea temperatures, weather and even jellyfish in the water. These information can be accessed by tourism offices and hoteliers, helping them (and therefore visitors) make smarter decisions about where to go and what to do \cite{bbc_ot}.
